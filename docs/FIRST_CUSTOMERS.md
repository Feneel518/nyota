# Nyota: first three paying weddings

Budget: ₹0 for marketing. Goal: three paying weddings and evidence of why families buy. Activity targets below are experiments, not forecasts. Hosting, payment processing, and other operating costs are separate.

## The offer

“Your Gujarati wedding, beautifully invited. Every function, venue directions, and family RSVPs in one WhatsApp link.”

Start with Gujarati families in the city where you have the strongest personal connections. Test the configured website price; the current code default is ₹1,999 for six calendar months from publication. Always quote the actual checkout price. Preview is free; publication is paid. Personally help the first couples complete setup, without promising custom artwork or features that do not exist.

## Before accepting payment

- Verify a complete live purchase, publication, WhatsApp share, guest RSVP, and owner export. Local simulation is not live verification.
- Public support email: feneelp@gmail.com. Set SUPPORT_EMAIL in the deployment environment if an older value overrides the code default.
- Supply the actual operator identity and finalize refund eligibility and financial-record retention. The policy pages still identify those decisions as unfinished. Do not hide that uncertainty with invented policy language.
- Open the invitation on a real Android and iPhone, including the browser used from WhatsApp. Review Gujarati wording with a native reader.

## Week 1: talk to people

Ask 20 personal contacts for introductions. Find 20 relevant local photographers, planners, and invitation designers. Work through the list individually, about five contacts a day. Use docs/customer-outreach.csv to track progress. Do not buy lists or send bulk unsolicited messages.

Warm introduction request:

> I’ve built Nyota, an animated Gujarati/English wedding invitation with function details, maps, and family RSVPs. I’m looking for my first three couples and will personally help with setup. Do you know someone getting married who might like to see a short demo?

Photographer or designer message (replace the bracketed detail with a real observation):

> Hi [name], I liked [specific detail] in your wedding work. I’ve built a Gujarati/English wedding invitation couples can share on WhatsApp, with animated functions and guest replies. Could I send you a 20-second example to see whether it would suit your clients?

If they agree, send https://www.nyotaa.app/demo and ask what their couples would need before using it. Do not promise commissions, exclusivity, or a partner portal. Start with one introduction.

## Week 2: watch the buying journey

Run five demos with people who have an upcoming wedding. Let them use their own phone. Ask them to find a theme, add names and an event, preview the invitation, and explain what they think they are buying. Watch where they pause; do not immediately explain the interface.

Ask: “What are you using for invitations now?”, “What would stop you sharing this with your family?”, and “Would you publish this at the displayed price?” Record the exact objection. Avoid asking only whether they like the design.

## Weeks 3–4: earn proof and referrals

Help paying customers publish and check that their guests can respond. Record setup/support minutes so you know whether the price covers the work. Request an honest testimonial and permission to show the invitation separately; neither should be a condition of support. Ask satisfied couples for one introduction.

## Three short videos to record

Use the fictional sample; never expose real guest responses or a private editor. Record the phone viewport with the built-in screen recorder. No paid editing tool is necessary.

1. **Opening:** 0–3 seconds: “This is what your wedding guests open.” 3–15 seconds: open the invitation and show a ceremony. 15–20 seconds: “Want to try it? DM DEMO.”
2. **Language:** show the same invitation switching from English to Gujarati. Caption: “One invitation. Two languages. Every generation invited.” End with the demo link.
3. **Practical details:** show Haldi, Sangeet, and wedding venues, then the RSVP form. Caption: “Every function and every reply, together.” Do not claim measured time savings.

## What to measure each week

Count contacted → replied → demo viewed → own preview created → paid → published. Track channel, objection, follow-up date, and support minutes. Keep personal lead data private; use a local copy of the blank CSV and do not commit real customer information.

- Few replies: change the target audience or opening message.
- Demos but no previews: investigate relevance, design, and setup friction.
- Previews but no purchases: ask about trust, missing requirements, and price.
- Purchases but heavy support: simplify the step that repeatedly needs help.

Follow up once after several days with a useful demo or answer. Stop if they decline. Spend the next development cycle on the most repeated obstacle from these conversations.
